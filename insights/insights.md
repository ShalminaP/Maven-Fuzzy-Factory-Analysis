# Maven Fuzzy Factory: Key Insights

1. **Traffic and orders grew, with holiday peaks.** From 2013 to 2014, sessions rose 107% and orders rose 126%. Orders peak in November and December; November and December 2014 had 1,985 and 2,314 orders, respectively.
2. **Conversion improved over time.** The dashboard talks about session-to-order conversion increasing from 4.14% in 2012 to 6.60% in 2013 and 7.22% in 2014. March 2012 and March 2015 are partial months; 2015's reported 8.44% is through 19 March and should not be compared with a full year.
3. **Channel performance depends on the measure.** Gsearch generated 316,035 sessions (66.8% of traffic), while organic search had the highest reported revenue per session ($4.53). Socialbook had low reported conversion (3.21%) and revenue per session ($2.08); the pilot campaign is separately reported at 1.08% conversion.
4. **Mobile is the clearest conversion opportunity.** Desktop conversion is reported at 8.50% versus 3.09% on mobile, while mobile accounts for 30.8% of sessions. The funnel also shows a major loss between a product-page view and adding an item to cart: 45.2% continue at that step overall. The funnel dashboard clearly shows that mobile performs below desktop at every funnel step.
5. **Order value and revenue per visit rose.** Average order value increased from $52.81 in 2013 to $63.80 in 2014 (about 21%). Revenue per session increased from $3.49 to $4.61 (about 32%). Two-item orders rose from 2.2% of orders in 2013 to 33.9% in 2014. Their timing is consistent with cross-selling contributing to order value, but does not prove causation.
6. **Product concentration and refunds merit review.** The Original Mr. Fuzzy accounts for 62.5% of item revenue and has a reported 5.11% refund rate. The Birthday Sugar Panda has the highest stated product refund rate at 6.04%; the overall item refund rate is 4.32%.
7. **Repeat purchasing is uncommon.** The analysis concludes that about 1.9% of customers purchased more than once.

## Suggested actions:

- Investigate the mobile journey, especially the transition from viewing a product to adding it to the cart.
- Protect branded search performance and review whether the Socialbook pilot should continue.
- Test cross-selling opportunities and track basket size and order value.
- Plan inventory and marketing budgets ahead of the November–December order peaks.
- Test post-purchase offers or email programs to encourage repeat purchases.

These are hypotheses to test, not proven fixes. The dataset has no advertising-spend field, so return on ad spend cannot be calculated; it also lacks location data. Channel classification includes analyst-defined rules, and the analysis is observational, so it describes patterns rather than proving their causes.
